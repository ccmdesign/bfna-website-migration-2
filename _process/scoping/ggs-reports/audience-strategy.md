# Introduction: How to Use This Document

This checklist provides a **shared framework** for making decisions about BFNA's website as the organization refocuses on an **audience-centric approach**.

**This document is intended to:**

- Help BFNA leadership prioritize what matters most on the website.
- Provide CCM Design with a clear mandate to recommend changes aligned with best practices.
- Establish guiding principles for clarity, accessibility, and engagement across the site.

**This document is not intended to:**

- Prescribe specific design or technical solutions.
- Offer case studies or page-by-page critiques.
- Replace the detailed user experience (UX) or copy recommendations that will follow.

**How to use the checklist:**

- Review each section as a decision filter when evaluating existing pages or planning updates.
- Use the checklist to align on priorities and trade-offs before implementation.
- Apply these principles consistently across pages, programs, and platforms.

# Section 1: Entry + Relevance

## Audience question: *Am I in the right place?*

> **Organizational decision:** We want people to have a clear, at-a-glance understanding of what BFNA is, what it does, and what problems it helps address.

## Entry + Relevance Checklist

When people first arrive at the BFNA website, they should be able to quickly determine whether the site is relevant to them and worth their time. This requires **clarity and prioritization**, not completeness.

- The site should clearly communicate **what BFNA is, what it focuses on, and the scope of its work** within moments of arrival.
- **Primary messages should be written for external audiences**, not for internal teams or programs.
- **Not all content is equally important at entry**; priority topics and work should be elevated over comprehensive listings.
- The site should avoid presenting **too many parallel pathways** at once, which can overwhelm new visitors.
- Language and framing should assume limited context and **avoid jargon**.
- Entry points should **emphasize relevance and purpose** before detail or depth.

# Section 2: Orientation + Way-Finding

## Audience question: *Where am I, and how does this site work?*

> **Organizational decision:** We'll prioritize consistency and predictability in navigation, labeling, and page structure so users can orient themselves quickly and move confidently through the site.

## Orientation + Way-Finding Checklist

Once visitors determine that BFNA is relevant to them, the site should help them understand where they are, how content is organized, and how to move through it without confusion or relearning patterns.

- Navigation systems are **clear, consistent, and predictable** across pages and devices.
- Page titles, headings, and labels clearly indicate **what a page contains and how it relates to the rest of the site** (and ideally the organization too).
- Similar types of pages use **consistent structure and naming conventions**, reducing the need for interpretation.
- Changes in context — such as links to **microsites or affiliated platforms** — are clearly signaled so users know when they're leaving the main site.
- Users can orient themselves without relying on visual cues alone; **structure and labeling support multiple ways of navigating**.
- Navigation and way-finding prioritize **helping users move forward**, not exposing the full complexity of the organization.

# Section 3: Choice + Cognitive Load

## Audience question: *What am I supposed to do next?*

> **Organizational decision:** We'll make intentional decisions about prioritization so users are guided toward meaningful next steps rather than being presented with too many equal options at once.

## Choice + Cognitive Load Checklist

An audience-centered website helps users move forward without requiring them to sort through every possible option. Reducing cognitive load is essential to clarity, accessibility, and engagement.

- Pages present a **limited number of clear, intentional choices** rather than many competing actions.
- **Not every program, platform, or initiative is given equal prominence**; priority is determined by audience needs and organizational goals.
- Calls to action are **specific and purposeful** rather than generic or repetitive.
- Users are guided toward **logical next steps** without being pushed to act before they're ready.
- Content is structured to support **progressive disclosure**, allowing users to go deeper when they choose.
- **Navigation and calls to action reflect clear content priorities** rather than compensating for a lack of prioritization.

# Section 4: Access + Inclusion

## Audience question: *Can I actually use this?*

> **Organizational decision:** We'll prioritize accessibility and inclusion so more people can access content, understand what's happening on the site, and complete key tasks without friction.

## Access + Inclusion Checklist

An audience-centered website removes barriers that prevent people from accessing information, navigating content, or completing actions. Accessibility supports usability, clarity, and trust for all users.

- The site supports **multiple ways of navigating and understanding content**, without relying on visual cues alone.
- Users can **move through the site and complete tasks without getting stuck**, lost, or disoriented.
- Interactive elements provide **clear feedback** so users know what has happened and what to do next.
- Links, downloads, and media clearly communicate **what they are and what to expect** before interaction.
- Content and interface decisions prioritize **readability, clarity, and ease of use** across devices and contexts.
- Accessibility considerations are treated as **core functionality**, not enhancements or add-ons.

# Section 5: Trust, Continuity + Exit

## Audience question: *Can I trust this — and do I want to come back?*

> **Organizational decision:** We'll design the site to reinforce credibility, neutrality, and continuity by making it easy for users to understand the organization, explore topics over time, and reengage on their own terms.

## Trust, Continuity + Exit Checklist

An audience-centered website builds trust by being consistent, transparent, and respectful of users' time and intentions. It supports continued engagement without pressure or persuasion.

- The site presents a **consistent and credible organizational presence** across pages, platforms, and content types.
- Content reinforces **BFNA's role as a nonpartisan, authoritative resource** without directing users toward advocacy or specific positions.
- Users are offered **clear, low-friction ways to continue engaging** with the organization, topics, or programs if they choose.
- Related content is surfaced in ways that support **ongoing learning** rather than forcing linear paths or outcomes.
- Transitions away from the site — whether to external platforms, downloads, or affiliated properties — are **clear and intentional**, allowing users to exit without confusion.
- The site respects that **not every visit results in immediate action** and treats partial engagement as a valid outcome.

# What's Next

This checklist sets the foundation for the next phase of work. Building on these principles, GGS will:

- Develop **site flow and UX recommendations** for a set of central pages, translating this framework into guidance around navigation, hierarchy, and calls to action.
- Provide **copy recommendations** for those pages to support clarity, neutrality, and audience understanding.

These next deliverables will move BFNA from shared principles to concrete, page-level recommendations while remaining aligned with the priorities outlined here.
