# France’s Litmus Test

_Published: 2024-06-05_

While left-wing politicians such as Raphaël Glucksmann have been making waves, the dynamic between the far-right Marine Le Pen camp and the French President Macron camp — who faced off in the national elections — has now spilled over into the European elections. At the time of writing, the [predicted](https://polls.euronews.com/?view=map&menu=polls&loaded-analyses=3&loaded-polls=3&loaded-news=4&country=fr) results portend a major upset for the French president’s coalition, RENEW Europe. RENEW is expected to lose six seats (they currently hold 23), while Identity & Democracy, the far-right National Rally’s coalition, is expected to gain ten seats (they currently hold 18.) No matter the final results, the presidential camp should draw lessons from the trends that emerged on the road to these elections. This will be necessary in order to course correct their approach accordingly prior to the 2027 presidential elections. __Lessons to learn__ In a poll carried out by IPSOS in March 2024, [52%](https://www.lemonde.fr/politique/article/2024/03/11/elections-europeennes-2024-les-enjeux-nationaux-priment-encore-pour-un-electeur-sur-deux_6221333_823448.html) of French citizens stated that they are going to vote above all to “manifest their support or opposition to the president and his government”. In that same poll, 53% of French citizens stated that they were voting on national questions while 47% were voting on European questions. Heading the far-right National Rally list is the Le Pen protégé, 28-year-old Jordan Bardella. Bardella’s charismatic persona has made him a [household name](https://www.politico.eu/article/can-anything-stop-the-french-far-rights-new-leader/) in French politics. The majority of his voter base [represent](https://www.leparisien.fr/elections/europeennes/bien-sur-quon-est-dans-le-duel-emmanuel-macron-remet-le-rn-dans-son-viseur-avant-les-europeennes-26-02-2024-L4GKNL74XRDZDCGLJDUKCVYQFU.php) a Euroskeptic demographic that want to use the European election ballot to protest the current government. Loic Signor, spokesperson for Macron’s party, [stated](https://www.leparisien.fr/elections/europeennes/bien-sur-quon-est-dans-le-duel-emmanuel-macron-remet-le-rn-dans-son-viseur-avant-les-europeennes-26-02-2024-L4GKNL74XRDZDCGLJDUKCVYQFU.php) that “Bardella wants to nationalize the ballot, it’s up to us to Europeanize it!” Yet, [current](https://www.lesechos.fr/elections/europeennes/sondage-europeennes-2024-tous-les-resultats-du-barometre-eurotrack-2090451) polling indicates that 30% will be voting for the National Rally, while only 17% will vote for Macron’s party, Renaissance. The [last](https://www.lefigaro.fr/politique/europeennes-2024-le-rassemblement-national-confirme-le-camp-presidentiel-respire-20240324) time a French electoral list in the European elections received above 30% was in 1984 under the leadership of Simone Veil, who was elected in 1979 as the first woman president of the European Parliament. 40 years later, the pendulum has swung to the far-right in France. It’s essential to understand what has won over the French electorate. __Poli-Tik(Tok)__ Globally, [44%](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2023) of 18 to 24-year-olds use TikTok as their primary news source. Across Europe, there are 142 million people who use the application. TikTok has become key to reach younger voters. This is no surprise to Jordan Bardella. He has 1.3 million followers on TikTok to date, and his content clearly resonates with his intended French audience. The polls indicate that only 4% of 18-24-year-olds plan on voting for Renaissance, while 31% are planning on voting for the National Rally. When Bardella posts, he mixes political discourse as well as light-hearted [“meme-ified”](https://www.tiktok.com/@jordanbardella/video/7315491283491900704) content.
