# The Nuclear Option

_Published: 2026-07-21_

Since 2022—amid a global energy crisis—comments from German leaders and public opinion polling have suggested that the country may be rethinking its attitude towards nuclear energy, with a growing number of Germans now open to the idea. For most German political parties, nuclear energy is a question of policy. For the foundationally anti-nuclear Green Party, however, this debate is existential. ## Germany’s Nuclear Energy History Born directly from the environmentalist movement, the Green Party is fundamentally unlike other German political parties. The party has its roots in the nationwide anti-nuclear protests of the 1980s and the 1990s. As the Chernobyl and Fukushima disasters in 1986 and 2011, respectively, increased public pressure to phase out nuclear energy, the Green Party mobilized to push for a nationwide nuclear exit ([Atomausstieg](https://us.boell.org/en/2023/04/21/understanding-german-nuclear-exit)) and simultaneously advocated for the adoption of renewable energy sources, primarily wind and solar. As support for the party intensified, it grew from a small opposition group to a mainstream governing party, forming its first federal coalition in the [late 1990s](https://www.boell.de/sites/default/files/assets/boell.de/images/download_de/publikationen/1998_Wahlprogramm_Bundestagswahl.pdf). The nuclear exit was ultimately successful, as [Germany closed its last nuclear reactor in 2023](https://www.base.bund.de/en/nuclear-safety/nuclear-phase-out/nuclear-phase-out_content.html), just as German public opinion on nuclear energy began to turn. Opposition to nuclear energy has historically been a unifying position in Germany, especially after the Fukushima disaster in 2011. According to polls taken that year, [nine out of 10 Germans](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-berlin-nuclear-is-over-the-debate-isnt/) supported a nuclear phase-out. However, recent polling suggests attitudes are changing. A survey conducted in 2025 found that only [36 percent opposed](https://www.dw.com/en/german-poll-majority-for-return-to-nuclear-energy/a-72139350) the return of nuclear energy outright, while a majority (55 percent) expressed support for its restoration. Recent shocks to European energy markets have heavily influenced this shift. The compounding effects of the energy crises in 2022 and 2026 signaled to German society that such challenges were not one-off incidents but a broader strategic issue to address. Spiraling energy costs have weighed heavily on the country’s economy, with German economists [more than halving](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c4g8k8vq8gno) their 2026 expected growth predictions. The resultant economic anxieties are directly affecting German society, with residents now more likely to cite [affordability](https://www.dw.com/en/germany-fears-living-costs-inflation-economy-ukraine-trump-graphics/a-74039463) and economic competitiveness as top priorities above environmentalism.
