## Design Specificity Verdict

~25% authored, ~75% category-interchangeable.

Genuinely specific: the line-drawn fellow portraits on /future-leadership/class-of-2026/; the four-colour programme system as an IA commitment; the rotated vertical left rail; the wordmark lockup.

Category-interchangeable: bleached full-bleed architecture photo + white scrim + navy headline (Pantheon oculus, Capitol dome, glass atrium — the default stock language of institutional trust); white card with faint grey shadow + image + title + 3-line excerpt + "Learn More"; dark navy 4-column footer with newsletter box; the typewriter adjective.

The damning evidence is internal: BFNA's own media is bolder than BFNA's website. The Transponder podcast art, the Lithium Rising poster, the About film title cards are all confident and typographically brave. The website is the timid outlier in its own brand.

Deterministic scan: CLI detector correctly skipped (URL target, per playbook). Browser overlay injection FAILED — DOM mutation works, but the harness hard-blocks localhost requests from a remote https origin (net::ERR_BLOCKED_BY_CLIENT on all four URL variants; live-server has no TLS mode). No user-visible overlay exists. All Assessment B findings are hand-measured instrumentation, not detector rule output.

## Corrections to the existing baseline study

This pass overturns four things recorded in the earlier surface study:

1. "Headings run semibold" — FALSE. Only three Typekit weights ship (300/400/700). There is no semibold face. Every heading set at 600 (h1, .card__heading, chips — 16 elements) is browser-synthesized faux-bold of the 700.
2. "Rotating announcement ticker" — FALSE. Computed position static, animation-name none, height 45px. It does not rotate and contributes zero layout shift.
3. Topic colour mapping may be REVERSED. Measured Democracy = green #4F8C71 and Politics & Society = orange #FA8900 — the opposite of the study. Settle before v2 tokens are written.
4. "Light body text needs a contrast check" — it PASSES, comfortably. Body p at weight 300 measures 10.63:1; newsletter copy 8.92:1. The risk in weight 300 is stroke thinness, not contrast. Contrast is not the failure mode there.

Also settled: CLS is 0.0000 on all four pages tested; FCP 416ms. Fonts are Adobe Typekit delivered via kit CSS pasted into an inline style block plus three preload links — which is why a source grep for @font-face found nothing. These are versioned Typekit URLs with a primer hash, so an Adobe-side rotation silently drops the site to Helvetica Neue with no error.

## Where the two assessments disagreed

- Horizontal overflow at mobile: A reported 13px of scroll (WCAG 1.4.10). B disproved it — 388 vs 375 is the scrollbar; forcing scrollLeft to 9999 left all three values at 0. No overflow. B wins. But A's underlying point survives: the off-canvas search inputs are pushed off-screen rather than removed and remain in the tab order (28 tabbable off-screen elements).
- Icon-link labels: A suspected the left rail. Wrong — the rail is correctly labelled. B found the real defect next door: .main-nav__button sets aria-label to the raw URL, so a screen reader announces "https://www.facebook.com/BertelsmannFoundation/". 8 per page.
- Unrendered "Our Platforms" panel: A saw an orphan heading in three headless captures, rendered fine live. B did not reproduce. INVESTIGATE, do not report as fact.

## What's Working — the five things that MUST survive

1. The article reading column. PORT. One family, 300 weight, 1.62 line-height, ~630px measure, 1.62 modular rhythm. The ratio means deck, body and subheads relate by one number, so nothing needs eyeballing. This is the site's actual craft.
2. The uppercase topic chip above card titles. PORT the device, DROP the markup. Fastest orientation cue in a mixed feed; makes the programme IA visible without a filter UI. But it must stop being an h2 — that single change removes ~330 phantom headings from /updates (673 h2 today).
3. The dark navy footer. PORT. The only block where hierarchy, contrast (7.41:1) and grouping are all correct at once. Treats the footer as a site map, not a legal dump.
4. The line-drawn fellow portraits. PORT and expand. The only asset no competitor could use. Warm, non-institutional, scales to any fellow without a photoshoot. Extend to team, authors, podcast guests — it is a brand asset hiding on one route.
5. The visually-hidden link context. PORT. Card CTAs read "Learn More about A Red Line for the Blue Wave" to assistive tech. Deliberate and correct; the one piece of genuine accessibility craft in the build.

Also structurally sound and worth inheriting conceptually: 58 of 59 homepage images carry srcset + sizes + loading=lazy; 0 missing alt; proper role=tabpanel + aria-labelledby on /updates.

## Priority Issues

### P0 — Site search is dead, and results have no URL. Verdict: DROP, rebuild.
"democracy" and "NATO" both return 0 Results from a site that publishes "How Important is NATO?". A TypeError (footerSearchSubmit.addEventListener is not a function) throws on every page load. The footer field sits in an hgroup with no form, so Enter does nothing either. Architecture is localStorage.setItem + window.location='/search/'.
Verified downstream consequence B found and A missed: the throwing statement sits inside a 4,924-character inline script block, and the code immediately after it — never reached — is the Embedly loader. The head preconnects to cdn.embedly.com, so embeds are expected and their initializer is unreachable on every page.
Fix: search is a real route in v2 — /search?q=&type=&topic=&from= — server-rendered from Directus, deep-linkable, faceted, with a designed empty state offering the programmes as escape hatches.

### P0 — The topic colours fail contrast at text sizes. Verdict: EVOLVE, keep the hues.
Measured, white text at 16px/400: orange #FA8900 2.43:1 FAIL (fails even AA large), green #4F8C71 3.95:1 FAIL normal, red #C53441 5.33:1 pass, purple #621764 11.50:1 pass. The four hues span a 4.7x contrast range, which is why purple reads as a heavy block and orange as a wash.
Worst instances: .button--primary "Learn More" at 2.43:1 across 13 instances — the site's main CTA. Inactive .tabs__item at 1.74:1, an interactive control. Article h1 orange at 2.43:1 while its own h2 subtitle below sits at 10.63:1 — hierarchy inverted by contrast, the eye lands on the subtitle.
Fix: split each topic into two tokens — an accent (current hue: rules, fills, chip backgrounds, large display) and an on-white text variant at >=4.5:1. Orange to roughly #8A4A00, green to about #3A6B52. Chips get dark-text-on-tint instead of white-on-saturated. Purple and red already pass and set the target. Add a light-on-dark variant too — it does not exist and video heroes will require it.

### P1 — Hero body copy runs off its own gradient onto the photograph. Verdict: EVOLVE.
Measured on /democracy at y=660: 10.55:1 at x=120, still 10.0:1 at x=660, then 3.07:1 at x=720, 2.76:1 at x=780, 2.21:1 at x=840. The scrim ends near x=700; the measure runs to x=795. The last words of every line fall onto raw glass. This is a programme landing page — where a funder or journalist arrives from a link — and its mission statement degrades to unreadable mid-sentence.
Fix: never size a scrim and a measure independently. Solid or heavily-tinted panel, or move copy below the image. If it overlays, the scrim is a token derived from the measure, not a hand-tuned gradient stop.

### P1 — In-article citations are invisible. Verdict: EVOLVE, and make it loud.
Link rgb(8,67,94) against body rgba(8,67,94,0.8) = 1.77:1 between them. WCAG 1.4.1 requires 3:1 when colour alone distinguishes. No underline, no weight change. The only difference is 20% opacity. /future-leadership does underline its inline links, so this is template inconsistency, not a decision.
For a think tank the visible chain of sources is the product. The reader cannot see that the article cites the BBC, Deutschlandfunk and Wahlrecht.de at all.
Fix: underline with a topic-coloured text-decoration-color and 0.15em offset; target=_blank rel=noopener on external sources. This is a place where "bolder" and "correct" are the same move.

### P1 — Focus is suppressed, and the first ring a keyboard user sees is off-screen. Verdict: DROP the reset, EVOLVE into a token.
Zero :focus-visible rules exist site-wide. Thirteen :focus rules exist and several explicitly remove the indicator (outline: 0 on .u-button, .button, .form button, .u-button-reset, .main-nav__button, .frame-button, .frame-search__*, .tabs__item, all form inputs). Probing 22 focusable elements: 9 show a ring, 13 do not. All six left-rail social links compute outline: none 0px. .off-canvas__close has no computed change whatsoever between states.
The only "replacement" on buttons is :focus sharing hover's 5% darkening — nowhere near the 3:1 WCAG requires for non-text.
The first visible ring a keyboard user encounters is tab stop 13, a.frame__logo.is-hidden at y=-300px — a stray rectangle at the top-left attached to nothing. No skip link, no main landmark on any page. 28 elements are tabbable while positioned entirely off-screen.
WCAG 2.4.7 failure.

### P2 — /updates ships everything at once. Verdict: EVOLVE the concept, DROP the implementation.
336 cards, 161 images, 673 h2 (chip + title per card), 839 headings and 746 links in one document, 585KB decoded HTML, zero pagination elements. Did not finish painting in a 9s budget at 1440px on a clean profile.
Fix: paginate or virtualise; chips become spans; desktop search controls get display:none at mobile rather than an off-canvas position; tabs become a scroll container with edge fades, or a select below 40em.

### P2 — Cards have no hover state at all. Verdict: EVOLVE.
No .card:hover rule exists anywhere. Cards compute cursor: auto and transition: all — a transition declared and never used. Card shadows are flat rgb(230,230,230), barely separating from white. Dozens of cards per page are the primary interactive object and they are inert.
Fix: one hover token — shadow lift, 1.02–1.03 image scale inside overflow:hidden, title shifts to the topic accent, a thin topic-coloured rule wipes along the top edge over 180ms. Identical treatment on :focus-visible. Replace transition: all with named properties.

### P2 — Performance defects worth fixing before they are inherited
Hero images are double-downloaded on every page: both a WebP and a JPEG are preloaded unconditionally while the picture element negotiates only one. Waste measured at 152KB on /, 647KB on /updates, 315KB on /about — 80–89% of hero bytes discarded.
Material Icons is 125.6KB, larger than all three brand font weights combined (55.6KB), for a handful of glyphs (arrow_back, menu, search, close). On the Lithium Rising article it is the single largest resource on the page.
Images are systematically UNDER-resolved, not oversized: at DPR 2, 8 of 8 measurable images fell below 1x device resolution (worst 0.34x). Two causes — the sizes attribute understates the real box (declares 320px, renders 470px, so the browser correctly picks the 320w candidate), and the hero has no srcset at all (a 1000px WebP into a 1367px box, with width=1600 height=1067 declared on the img).

## The DROP list

1. The JS loading curtain (.loading-container + site-wrap opacity:0 + four fading BFNA letters). A content site must never hide itself behind script.
2. The hero typewriter. Infinite loop, no pause (WCAG 2.2.2), no aria-hidden or aria-live, no reduced-motion guard, and a hard br after the span makes the sentence oscillate between two and three lines forever. The most SaaS gesture on a think-tank site.
3. outline:0 / outline:none with no replacement. Ten selectors.
4. The --colors-* token layer. Live values include --colors-default: $black and --colors-teal: $teal (unresolved SCSS leaked into custom properties), --colors-alt: --colors-teal (a variable name as a value, missing var()), --color-black: hsla(0, 0%, 15% 1) (missing comma, invalid), and --white-05-hsl: 0, 0%, 05% which is black named white. Inert garbage.
5. The BEM-modifier theming mechanism (hero--digital-world, frame-nav--digital-world, product-card--digital-world, button--digital-world, card--politics-society...). No --theme-hsl on root or body, so every new component must be taught all four programmes. In v2 set the token once on the route wrapper and inherit.
6. The end-of-article Print button as the sole terminal action.
7. The duplicated off-canvas menu — 21 links emitted twice, 43 anchors, announced twice, 42 tab stops.
8. Off-canvas and desktop search controls left in the tab order while hidden.
9. hgroup class="field-group" wrapping an input and button; the stray mobile="" attribute; the vestigial data-v-111d9425 Vue scope attribute on a static page.
10. alt="Highlight card image" on 59 homepage images — worse than empty, because it is announced.
11. The two competing label systems. Keep the chip, retire the "Podcast | Title" pipes.
12. Duplicate IDs: filter-democracy, filter-politics-society, filter-future-leadership, filter-digital-world each x3, plus empty id="" x3. Only 15 IDs exist on the homepage and 5 are duplicated; label[for] binds only the first match, so two of three filter groups are effectively mislabelled.
13. Unlabelled form controls: 7 of 20 on the homepage, 4 of 4 on /about and the article — three search inputs (placeholder-only), a submit, three selects. No label, no aria-label, no aria-labelledby.
14. aria-label set to the raw URL on 8 .main-nav__button social links per page.
15. Inconsistent social sets — 6 in the rail, 4 in the footer.
16. The German newspaper collage hero on /updates — semantically unrelated to transatlantic policy, and its newsprint competes with the page's own headline.
17. Tap targets below the WCAG 2.5.8 24x24 minimum: 7 at mobile (.tabs__item 100x21 x4, .main-nav__link 79x17 and 87x19, .link 119x20).

Falls back to /updates, where the topic select is below a 1400px hero, tab labels clip at mobile, and 206 publications have no date sort, no year filter, no author filter and no pagination. Opens a candidate, wants to check sourcing — 18 citations at 1.77:1 against body text, so she cannot tell which claims are sourced without hovering every phrase. Breaks at: .search-input, .tabs__list, p a.

Journalist on deadline: lands on an article from a tweet; the reading experience is genuinely good. Then the h1 at 2.43:1 is hard to read on a bright screen. Even if it worked, the URL would be /search/ with no query, so she could not send results to her editor. Breaks at: h1, .footer-search__submit, /search/, article footer.

Prospective fellowship applicant: /future-leadership/class-of-2026/ is the best page on the site — breadcrumb, red h1 at a passing 5.33:1, clear description, excellent portraits. Then it stops. ## The Boldness Gap

1. The hero is 100vh of empty ceiling. Top ~40% of the homepage viewport is blown-out masonry with nothing on it; the headline sits at ~44% height. The photographs are bleached rather than styled, so they read as apology. Confident version: crop hard and go dark — a real 60% navy multiply, wordmark reversed white, 4.5rem headline at the optical centre. Or drop the photo on Operate routes and open /updates with a typographic masthead: "206 publications. Four programmes. Since 2008."
2. Four brand colours used at 12px. The entire chromatic identity appears as a tag. Confident version: let the programme colour own the page — a full-bleed coloured band behind the hub headline, 4px coloured rules between article sections, a coloured vertical spine down the article's left edge telling you which programme you are reading. Purple at 11.5:1 can carry white text at any size — use it as a surface, not a sticker.
3. The type scale never reaches the top of its own range. Eleven steps on a 1.62 ratio, and the largest thing on an article page is a 32px h1. The spec allows 4.5rem. Confident version: article h1 at 4–4.5rem, 0.95 leading, ranged left against the same measure as the body so the column edge is a hard vertical. That single change makes the article look like a publication rather than a blog post.
4. Cards that do not react to being touched. See P2.
5. Nothing marks where you are. Nav never shows active state, rail never highlights, filters leave no chip. Confident version: the active programme's nav item takes a solid underline in its own hue; the rail label goes solid; the filter renders as a dismissible coloured chip above the grid. Orientation as ornament.

Strategic point: the bolder version already exists inside the brand — in the Transponder artwork, the Lithium Rising poster, the About film's title cards. The website is not under-branded; it is the only place the brand whispers. v2's job is to bring the site up to the confidence of BFNA's own media, not to invent a new voice.

## Video-Hero Compatibility (Higgsfield)

Survives as-is: the wordmark reversed to white (a bfna-logo--white variant already exists and the solid FOUNDATION bar is a built-in backing plate — the most motion-ready element on the site); the headline at 4rem+ in white with a real scrim; the announcement bar (it sits above the hero media in the DOM, never overlays).

Breaks and must be re-engineered:
- The white-gradient scrim. Hand-tuned to one still, and it already fails on a still (10.55:1 to 2.21:1 across one line on /democracy). Against changing luminance it fails continuously and unpredictably. Motion needs a fixed-opacity scrim — 55–65% navy multiply — or a solid content panel.
- Navy text over the hero. /, /about, /democracy and /updates all put #08435E body copy on the image. Navy-on-video is not survivable at any scrim setting. Hero copy must invert to white, which means every topic colour needs a light-on-dark variant that does not currently exist.
- The floating "Our Platforms" panel. Today a bare h3 plus four buttons with no background over the right third of the image; already invisible against dark glass on /democracy. Over video, unreadable in principle. Needs an opaque or heavily-blurred surface, or to move out of the hero.
- The typewriter accent word. Orange at 2.43:1 on a still white ceiling; over video it disappears, and a text animation competing with a moving background is two motions fighting. Drop it.
- Full-viewport hero height. Acceptable for a still, punishing for video. Cap at 60–70vh with the first content row peeking above the fold.
- prefers-reduced-motion. Exactly one such rule exists site-wide and it covers the preloader's .letter. Meanwhile 105 elements carry active transitions on the homepage, and .site-wrap itself animates opacity and visibility on every page transition, unguarded. A video hero requires a real reduced-motion path — poster frame, paused, with a play control. That plumbing does not exist.

## Minor Observations

- Contentful images carry proper srcset and sizes on cards; that competence PORTs conceptually to Directus. 26 homepage images are served as WebP via ?fm=webp despite .jpg path extensions, so an extension-based format audit would produce a false "38 legacy JPEGs" finding.
- .card__brow letter-spacing is normal while .tabs__item uses 0.6px — two uppercase treatments, two tracking values.
- The footer Subscribe button is green #4F8C71, a programme colour, in a global topic-neutral component.
- "Contact" is filed under the "Updates" footer column.
- Footer column 1 has no heading while columns 2 and 3 do; its links are bold white 17.6px while theirs are light grey 14.96px. Two link treatments in one row.
- /updates tab panels carry proper role=tabpanel and aria-labelledby, but tabs lack aria-controls and there is no roving tabindex — the declared ARIA pattern is a promise the implementation does not keep.
- One h1 per page, correct. But on every page the h1 sits at DOM position 4–5, preceded by H3 Topics, H3 Updates, H4 Subscribe, H3 Our Platforms. The document opens at H3.
- The article page has a real 2 to 4 heading skip plus two empty headings.
- footer is used as a card sub-element 74 times on the homepage and 338 times on /updates, not as the site landmark.
- Newsletter goes to Mailchimp, external, no inline capture.
- lang, viewport, OG tags and meta description all present and correct.

## False Positives Ruled Out

- .site-wrap opacity:0 / visibility:hidden looked like broken rendering but is the page-transition fade captured mid-navigation. Not a bug.
- Images are not oversized — the usual flag does not apply here; every measurable image was under-resolved.
- The weight-300 contrast sweep initially caught style, script and noscript elements as "text at weight 300"; excluded.
- 15 legacy JPEG YouTube thumbnails are third-party i.ytimg.com assets, not fixable site-side.
- The /democracy "2 x h1" reading came from an unreliable run. Unconfirmed.
